What changes

  • One decision, one column: the two “Allow” buttons stacked at full width, read only first and primary, marked Recommended; read and write under it, secondary. Nothing wraps at any width, because nothing sits side by side.
  • “Deny” is apart: under a rule, a quiet button at the right, beside who is signed in. It can't be mistaken for the third of three equal choices, and it is never the default.
  • The text is cut in four parts, each with its own heading: who asks and where it goes back to (the address in mono, so it can be checked), what it will read, what write would allow (only when asked, in the warning tint), and how to revoke it.
  • Only read asked (the switch above): the write part and the second button go; one “Allow read only” and Deny remain.
  • Phone: the page is the screen, no card. The text scrolls; the choices stay at the bottom, Deny last and quiet.
  • No gap: the card is as tall as its content, centred, with the brand above it, as on the sign-in screen.

Rejected

  • Three buttons on one row (today): “Allow read only” alone, then the two others wrapped under it.
  • Deny as a red button: refusing is safe, not destructive; red would make it the loudest choice.
  • Checkboxes per right (read accounts, read lines, write…): the server has two scopes; boxes would promise a finer control that does not exist.
  • Read and write as the primary: the safer choice is the easy one.

Motion

Press

Reduced
The coin pulses instead of falling.
